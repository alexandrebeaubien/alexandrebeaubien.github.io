  • 2024.10 - 2025.08

    Tsukuba, Japan

    Research stay: polarized electron source R&D
    KEK
    KEK–TRIUMF EPECR Fellow (Exchange Program for Early Career Researchers), Jan – Mar 2025.
    • Designed a GaAs-based polarized electron source, from concept to final design and manufacturing, for the Touschek experiment: a polarization-lifetime measurement in the SuperKEKB High Energy Ring and the first step toward the proposed Chiral Belle upgrade. Drew its ultra-high-vacuum chamber, selected the components and assisted the KEK technicians with the assembly and bake-out.
    • Optimized the cathode and anode in simulation to minimize the emittance, reaching about 1.6 mm·mrad; developed the electron-beam transport simulations and designed the focusing magnets.
    • Designed and simulated a prototype beam merger to integrate the source into the beamline.
    • Supported the beam-tracking simulations: measured the dimensions and magnetic fields of the injection room, produced drawings of the room, obtained the simulation input files and coordinated between KEK and UVic.
    • Presented the work in an invited seminar at TRIUMF and at the CAP 2025 Congress.
  • 2022.01 - Present

    Victoria, BC, Canada

    Doctoral research, Belle II
    University of Victoria and the Belle II Collaboration
    • Measurement of the e⁺e⁻ → π⁺π⁻ cross section at the SuperKEKB collision energy (10.58 GeV) to extract the pion electromagnetic form factor; sole analyst, using the 365 fb⁻¹ Run 1 Υ(4S) dataset; the analysis is close to the working-group review stage. Regularly presented at Belle II working-group meetings and at General Meetings since 2024.
    • Built a custom BDT-based particle identification tool for pions at the full beam energy.
    • Measured the BDT efficiency from data for each particle type, as two-dimensional angular maps to measure the detector’s systematic effects: pions from τ → (ρ → ππ⁰)ν decays for the signal, and electrons and muons from e⁺e⁻ and μ⁺μ⁻ control samples for the backgrounds.
    • Studied the systematic effects of detector mis-modelling: in the KLM, removing regions with poor sensitivity from the fiducial region; in the ECL, for electron discrimination; and in the track momentum.
    • Redesigned the ECL and support-structure geometries in the Belle II GEANT4 simulation to resolve ECL-related volume overlaps.
    • Extended the MSc GAN waveform generator to all 8736 ECL crystals (one model per crystal) and wrote a prototype basf2 module (eclBGOverlaySimulator, not yet in a release) to use generated noise in simulation; presented at Belle II General Meetings (2023, 2024) and the CAP 2023 Congress.
    • Developed a multivariate classifier that separates BB̄ from cc̄ events using event-level kinematic and topology variables, in order to study the physics program of the Chiral Belle upgrade.

  • 2019.09 - 2021.12

    Victoria, BC, Canada

    MSc research, Belle II electromagnetic calorimeter
    University of Victoria and the Belle II Collaboration
    • Developed a deep convolutional generative adversarial network (GAN) that generates noise waveforms for the ECL crystals, trained on 154,692 recorded waveforms. Generating noise for all 8736 crystals of one event takes 7.7% of the time needed to simulate and reconstruct a BB̄ event.
    • Developed a charged-particle identification tool based on ECL pulse-shape discrimination (PSD), with gradient-boosted decision trees trained on one million simulated particles per species: electron–pion AUC improved from 0.980 to 0.996 overall and from 0.928 to 0.978 below 1 GeV/c, outperforming basf2’s PSD classifier trained on neutral clusters. Presented at the CAP 2022 Congress; re-implemented during the PhD with 1.8 million simulated tracks, confirming the gain.
